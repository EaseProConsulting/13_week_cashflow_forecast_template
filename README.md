The forecast is built from a small set of business assumptions:

| Input              | Purpose                                                                          |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start Date         | Sets the beginning of the 13-week forecast                                       |
| Opening Cash       | Starting available cash balance                                                  |
| Monthly Revenue    | Current recurring revenue assumption                                             |
| Revenue Growth     | Expected month-on-month growth                                                   |
| COGS %             | Estimated cost of goods sold as a percentage of revenue                          |
| AR Days            | Average time taken to collect customer receivables                               |
| AP Days            | Average time taken to pay vendors                                                |
| Recurring Expenses | Rent, payroll, software, utilities, debt payments and other recurring cash costs |

Recurring expenses can be assigned different payment frequencies depending on how the expense is actually paid.

## Why Use a 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast?

Profitability and liquidity are not the same thing.

A business may be profitable on paper while still experiencing cash pressure because customer collections, payroll, supplier payments and other cash movements happen at different times.

A **13-week cash flow forecast** provides a practical short-term view of expected liquidity and can support better cash management and payment planning.

It can help you:

* Identify potential cash shortfalls earlier
* See when your cash balance may reach its lowest point
* Plan supplier and operating payments
* Understand the effect of AR and AP timing
* Improve short-term liquidity visibility
* Support working capital planning
* Make more informed cash management decisions
